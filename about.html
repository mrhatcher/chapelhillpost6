Post 6 Today

A New Chapter in a 100-Year Story

Post No. 6's modern facility at 3700 NC Highway 54 West sits on a 128-acre site west of Chapel Hill. The building features a full gym, games room, bar, ample dining and meeting space, and expansive outdoor areas.

Our strategic location draws members from Chapel Hill, Durham, Burlington, and outlying areas. The facility serves over 15 organizations, from VFW Post 9100 to Boy Scout troops to the Chapel Hill Fire and Police departments.

When National Commander Paul Dillard visited on February 17, 2022, he called the facility "wonderful" and praised the Post's commitment to its members and community.

Preamble to the Constitution of the American Legion

"For God and Country we associate ourselves together for the following purposes: To uphold and defend the Constitution of the United States of America; to maintain law and order; to foster and perpetuate a one-hundred percent Americanism; to preserve the memories and incidents of our association in the Great Wars; to inculcate a sense of individual obligation to the community, state and nation; to combat the autocracy of both the classes and the masses; to make right the master of might; to promote peace and good will on earth; to safeguard and transmit to posterity the principles of justice, freedom and democracy; to consecrate and sanctify our comradeship by our devotion to mutual helpfulness."

Our Foundation

Four Pillars of Service

The American Legion stands on four core pillars. Post No. 6 lives these values every day.

01

Veterans Affairs & Rehabilitation

Ensuring every veteran receives the care and benefits they have earned through their service. The American Legion has been the leading advocate for veterans' rights since its founding in 1919.

Post No. 6 provides direct assistance through our accredited Veterans Service Officer (VSO), William Munsee, who helps members and non-members navigate VA claims, disability compensation, pension benefits, and healthcare enrollment — all at no charge. Contact him at vso@alpost6.org.

02

National Security

Supporting a strong, well-prepared national defense. The American Legion believes that a robust military capability is essential to preserving peace and protecting the freedoms that veterans fought to defend.

Post No. 6 advocates for adequate defense funding, proper care for active-duty service members, and policies that honor the sacrifices of those who wear the uniform today and those who have worn it in the past.

03

Americanism

Promoting patriotism, youth programs, and civic responsibility throughout the community. Americanism means fostering love of country, respect for the flag, and active participation in the democratic process.

Post No. 6 promotes Americanism through our Boys State scholarship program, the Oratorical Contest, flag retirement ceremonies, the Speakers' Bureau, and community events that celebrate American values and history.

04

Children & Youth

Investing in the next generation through scouting, Boys State, and scholarships. The American Legion believes that strong youth programs are the foundation of a strong nation.

Post No. 6 sponsors Boy Scout Troops 505 and Crew 505, funds Boys and Girls State leadership scholarships, hosts the annual Oratorical Contest, and supports youth baseball — giving Chapel Hill's young people the skills, values, and opportunities they need to become tomorrow's leaders.

Our History

Over 100 Years of Service

From a hand-built log cabin to a modern 128-acre facility, Post 6 has been serving Chapel Hill since 1919.

1919 — Chartered

Chapel Hill Post No. 6 was chartered on October 28, 1919, making it one of the earliest American Legion posts in North Carolina. WWI veterans returning to Chapel Hill organized the Post to continue serving their community.

The First Post Hut

WWI veterans built the Post's first home — a log cabin on Rosemary Street — by hand. It served as the center for veteran activities and community gatherings for decades.

1962-2019 — Legion Road Era

The Post moved to 1714 Legion Road, where it served the community for 57 years. In 2016, the Town of Chapel Hill purchased the 36-acre property for $7.9 million, allowing the Post to build its modern facility.

2019 — A New Home

Post 6 opened its new facility at 3700 NC Highway 54 West on a 128-acre site. The modern building features a full gym, games room, bar, commercial kitchen, and ample meeting space — a home worthy of the Post's legacy.

Stories From Our Past

Historical Stories

Centennial Celebration (2018-2019)

To mark 100 years of service, Post 6 hosted a centennial event featuring professional actor David Shuey portraying General John J. Pershing. About 100 attendees gathered to celebrate the Post's century of commitment to veterans and community.

Dachau Ashes Memorial (2014-2015)

In a solemn ceremony, ashes from the Dachau concentration camp ovens — brought home by a WWII soldier — were buried at Durham Hebrew Cemetery. Post Commander Heavlin and Adjutant Gill attended to honor the memory of those lost in the Holocaust.

P.H. Craig Vehicle Collection

Post member P.H. Craig donated his collection of historic vehicles to the NC Transportation Museum, including a 1956 Chrysler and a WWII-era Jeep. The collection preserves both automotive and military history for future generations.

Veterans in Our Community

Post 6 veterans have been builders of Chapel Hill — doctors, lawyers, judges, engineers, carpenters, writers, and speakers who shaped the town we know today.

Under the GI Bill, Post 6 veterans became congressmen, judges, doctors, and shopkeepers. Many owned businesses in Eastgate, Rams Plaza, and downtown Chapel Hill. Today, over 500 veterans attend UNC Chapel Hill, continuing the tradition of service and education that defines our community.
