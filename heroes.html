Builders of Chapel Hill

Post 6 members are the builders of Chapel Hill — doctors, lawyers, judges, engineers, carpenters, writers, and speakers who shaped the town we know today.

From World War II to today's conflicts, our members have served their country and then returned home to serve their community. Under the GI Bill, Post 6 veterans became congressmen, judges, doctors, lawyers, and shopkeepers. Many owned businesses in Eastgate, Rams Plaza, and downtown Chapel Hill. Today, over 500 veterans attend UNC Chapel Hill, continuing this legacy of service and education.

Featured Veterans

Veterans Who Define Post No. 6

Jim & June Mackorell

Jim and June Mackorell have been pillars of Chapel Hill Post No. 6 for decades. Jim's military service and his continued dedication to the Post's mission reflect the best of what the American Legion stands for.

Together, Jim and June have devoted countless hours to veteran outreach, community events, and supporting fellow members through hardship. Their partnership is a testament to the Legion's commitment to family, service, and community.

Lee Heavlin

Lee Heavlin is a distinguished veteran and long-time member of Post No. 6 whose service to both country and community has left an indelible mark on Chapel Hill.

After his military career, Lee channeled his dedication into local veteran advocacy, helping fellow veterans navigate benefits and ensuring their stories are heard and honored.

Major Everett "Bud" Hampton

Major Everett "Bud" Hampton served his country with distinction, and his legacy continues through the work of Post No. 6 in Chapel Hill. His leadership during his military service set the standard for those who followed.

Major Hampton's contributions to the Post — from ceremonial duties to mentoring younger veterans — exemplify the spirit of service above self that defines the American Legion's highest ideals.

Robert Bauman

Robert Bauman's military career and subsequent service to Post No. 6 have made him one of Chapel Hill's most respected veterans. His commitment to honoring fellow veterans through the Post's Honor Guard is widely recognized.

Robert continues to be an active voice for veterans' rights and a bridge between the Post and the broader Chapel Hill community, ensuring that the American Legion's mission reaches those who need it most.

Dr. James R. Dingfelder

Dr. James R. Dingfelder has combined a distinguished professional career with a deep commitment to veteran service at Post No. 6. His unique perspective as both a veteran and a medical professional has been invaluable to the Post.

Dr. Dingfelder has been instrumental in connecting veterans with health resources and advocating for improved veteran healthcare in the Chapel Hill area, bridging the gap between military service and civilian medical care.

Nominate a Hero

Do you know a veteran or service member from the Chapel Hill area whose story deserves to be told? Post No. 6 is always looking to honor the men and women who have served our country.

To nominate someone for our "Our Heroes" spotlight, contact the Post adjutant with the veteran's name, branch of service, and a brief summary of their service and community contributions.
